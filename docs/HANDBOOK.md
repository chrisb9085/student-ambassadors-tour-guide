## About the FIU Foundation

The FIU Foundation is the university’s nonprofit fundraising organization. Through the support of alumni, donors, corporate partners, and community leaders, the Foundation helps secure philanthropic gifts that enhance the FIU experience. These contributions support scholarships, student success initiatives, research, academic programs, and campus improvements.

The Foundation is governed by a Board of Directors whose members provide leadership, advocacy, and financial support to advance FIU’s mission and strategic priorities.

<!-- p. 3 -->
## The Role of Student Ambassadors

As representatives of the FIU Foundation, Student Ambassadors serve as the student's voice in philanthropy and donor engagement efforts. Ambassadors help showcase the impact of donor support by sharing their FIU experiences, assisting with Foundation events, leading campus tours, and fostering meaningful connections between students, alumni, donors, and university partners.

Through professionalism, leadership, and service, Student Ambassadors help strengthen the relationship between the FIU community and those who invest in its future.

## How to Start a Tour

The first few minutes of a tour are critical in creating a welcoming and engaging experience. As the tour guide, your enthusiasm and professionalism set the tone for the entire visit.

### Welcome Your Guests

- Introduce yourself by sharing your name, major, year, and involvement on campus. This helps visitors connect with you as a current student and provides credibility to your experience.
- Thank guests for taking the time to visit FIU and acknowledge any families, faculty/staff, or special guests in attendance.

### Set Expectations

- Briefly explain the tour route, how long the tour will last, and the major locations that will be visited. This helps guests feel informed and comfortable throughout the experience.
- Encourage questions throughout the tour and remind guests that their visit is intended to be interactive.

### Create a Connection

- Ask guests where they are visiting from or what interests them most about FIU. This allows you to personalize information and build rapport with the group.
- Share a brief personal story about why you chose FIU or a memorable experience you have had as a student.

<!-- p. 5 -->
## Tips & Tricks

Successful tours are built on engagement, storytelling, and adaptability. While facts and statistics are important, guests are most likely to remember the stories and experiences that bring the campus to life.

### Be Conversational

- Speak naturally and avoid sounding scripted. Guests should feel like they are having a conversation with a current student rather than listening to a presentation.
- Make eye contact and include all members of the group when speaking.

### Use Personal Experiences

- Connect campus resources, traditions, and programs to your own experiences whenever possible. Personal examples help guests envision themselves at FIU.
- Share stories that highlight student success, involvement opportunities, and campus life.
- Pay attention to the interests of your guests. Some visitors may be most interested in academics, while others may have questions about housing, student life, or career preparation.
- Maintain Professionalism
- If you do not know the answer to a question, be honest and offer to connect the guest with the appropriate resource.
- Remain positive, respectful, and professional at all times, remembering that you are representing FIU.

## Charles Perry (PC)

Primera Casa, meaning "First House" in Spanish, was the first building constructed on FIU's Modesto A. Maidique Campus and opened when the university welcomed its first students in 1972.

In 1994, the building was renamed the Charles E. Perry Building in honor of FIU's founding president, Dr. Charles "Chuck" Perry, whose vision helped establish FIU as South Florida's public research university.

### Key Offices and Departments

- Office of the President
- Office of the Provost
- Academic departments and administrative offices
- Human Resources

### Building Highlights

- One of FIU's most historic buildings
- Serves as a center for university leadership and administration
- Features Las Cuatro Razas ("The Four Races"), FIU's first public art installation, located on the building's exterior.

> **Tour Guide Tip:** Primera Casa represents FIU's beginnings. When the university opened in 1972, much of campus life was centered in this building. Today, it remains a symbol of FIU's growth from a new university serving South Florida to a globally recognized research institution.

The College of Arts, Sciences & Education is FIU’s largest academic college, serving more than 26,000 students across the sciences, humanities, education, and social sciences. - **The School of Integrated Sciences & Humanity** brings together the natural and physical sciences with social sciences and mathematics. Areas of study include chemistry, physics, biochemistry, mathematics, psychology, statistics, and related disciplines that explore both the natural world and human behavior.
- **The School of Education & Human Development** prepares future educators, counselors, and leaders in human services. Programs include teacher education, counseling, school psychology, educational leadership, and recreation and human development.

Research within CASE spans multiple disciplines and often connects science, education, and society. Faculty and students engage in work that addresses environmental sustainability, human behavior, education systems, and community development.

### Student Profile
